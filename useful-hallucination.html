Useful Hallucination

John Stewart wishes there was an information ecosystem — one where people could access truth and act wisely. It's a noble desire, but it crashes against the oldest, weirdest wall in the stack:

All consciousness is made-up, from very improper impressions made upon our sense organs.

The cortex doesn’t see truth. It stitches together noise. Our minds are recursive narrators, not accurate reporters. There is no clean data. Only symbolic guesses that we build feedback loops around.

Faust Always Wins (For a While)

Marx knew: systems built on lies can outcompete systems built on care. Adversarial dynamics select for speed and deception. The Faustian bargain is the default. Trust is the rebellion.

No Clean Pipelines — Only Loops

We don’t need ecosystems of perfect data. We need epistemic humility. Relational feedback. Useful hallucinations that evolve in the light of better care.

🧠 Useful Hallucination Loop:
Sense → Hallucinate → Narrate → Believe → Act → Feedback → 🧠

Trust Over Truth

Truth is an unstable endpoint. Trust is the recursive engine. To design for trust means to accept uncertainty and keep returning — again and again — not to the data, but to the relationship that makes meaning possible.
