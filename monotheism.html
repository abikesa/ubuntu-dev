Structural Critique of the Original Argument

1. Forest/Tree Metaphor Collapse

Critical Flaw: The analogy begins with a reasonable premise (Judaism as key historical trunk of monotheism) but degenerates into incoherent imagery. The "burning bush" conclusion doesn't follow from the initial arboreal framework - a bush isn't a tree, and the Exodus theophany was explicitly non-destructive.
Superior Reformulation: "Monotheism's development resembles a mycorrhizal network more than a single tree - what appears as competitive destruction is actually nutrient exchange through divine hiddenness (hester panim)."

2. Theological Mischaracterization

The "jealous God" critique (Exodus 20:5) ignores the theological distinction between metaphysical jealousy (kina'ah as exclusive divine claim) and psychological jealousy. Maimonides clarifies this in Guide for the Perplexed 1:36 - divine "jealousy" describes ontological exclusivity, not emotional insecurity.
More Precise Objection: "A truly omnipotent God wouldn't require prohibitions against idolatry unless monotheism emerged through competitive cultural selection rather than revelation."

3. Missing Historical Layers

The original analysis overlooks the evolutionary stages of Yahweh worship from henotheism (Deuteronomy 32:8-9) to monotheism (Isaiah 45:5). The textual evidence shows gradual development, not instantaneous emergence.
Stronger Framing: "The Torah's editorial layers (JEDP sources) reveal monotheism as a branching process within its own tradition before confronting external religions."

Epistemic Improvements

1. Bayesian Historical Analysis

P(Monotheism|Cultural Evolution) vs P(Monotheism|Revelation)

The rapid consolidation of monotheism during the Babylonian exile (6th century BCE) represents an unlikely cultural transition without positing some revelatory prior.

2. Game Theoretic Perspective

Model religious development as an iterated game where monotheism's "jealousy" is an evolutionarily stable strategy:
  • Defection (idolatry) → cultural dissipation
  • Cooperation (exclusive worship) → high-group cohesion

3. Information Theory Application
